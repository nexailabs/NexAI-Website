# Toolkit heroes — Candidate 01, Direct-Response

**Angle:** Lead each hero with what the reader walks away with, and earn it with one kind of proof only — it ran on real work before we published it.
The author is irrelevant; the evidence isn't. Open door, not a pitch: "take this, it already shipped."

---

## App Vault

- **Eyebrow:** `App Vault · {N} tools`
- **Headline (H1):** `The software we *actually* run the business on.`
- **Sub:** `Every tool we use, what it replaced, and why it stayed. We picked it, we tested it, we wrote it up. No affiliate links, no roundup filler — just the stack that's in production right now.`

*Why: headline keeps the "actually" tension but trades the vaguer "stack" for "run the business on" so the benefit (this is real, not a listicle) lands in one beat. Sub leads with what the reader gets ("every tool, what it replaced, why it stayed") and uses the proof frame ("in production right now") instead of describing the page.*

---

## Prompt Hub

- **Eyebrow:** `Prompt Hub`
- **Headline (H1):** `*[Prompts | SOPs | skills.md]* we ran on real client work before we published them.`
  *(first word rotates: **Prompts** / **SOPs** / **skills.md** — mechanic preserved)*
- **Sub:** `Single-shot prompts, human runbooks, and skills your agent installs. Copy them, run them, ship faster. Some free, some paid.`

*Why: kept the battle-tested wedge but swapped "survived a month of real work" for "ran on real client work before we published them" — same "this shipped in production" proof, no clock. Dropped the payment vendor: "some free, some paid" carries the meaning; the processor is noise. Sub adds the three benefit verbs ("copy them, run them, ship faster") so the reader sees the payoff before the price note.*

---

## Field Notes

- **Eyebrow:** `Field Notes`
- **Headline (H1):** `Essays, playbooks, and *build logs* from the agents we ship.`
- **Sub:** `Everything here ran on real client work before it became a post — what worked, what broke, and what we'd do differently. No ghostwriters, no SEO filler, no hot takes.`
- **Signature line:** `from the lab in Bengaluru.`

*Why (the "proof not author" fix): the old sub opened "Written by the founders" — a credential this ICP discounts. Replaced it with the evidence: "everything here ran on real client work before it became a post." That's the same trust the toolkit sells everywhere — published only after it survived production — now stated as proof, not pedigree. Headline tightened "a company run on agents" to "the agents we ship" — sharper and concrete (the build logs come straight off live agent work), without contorting into "runs on its own agents." Kept the three "no" beats; they're a clean true line, so they stay.*

## What I'd A/B test next

1. Field Notes headline: "from the agents we ship" vs. a flatter "from the work, not the marketing team." — does naming the agents pull harder than naming the anti-pattern?
2. Field Notes sub: proof-first ("everything here ran on real client work…") vs. the three "no" beats first — test which clause earns the scroll for a fluff-allergic practitioner.
3. Prompt Hub sub: with benefit verbs ("copy them, run them, ship faster") vs. plain description — does the practitioner tail convert better on payoff or on restraint?
